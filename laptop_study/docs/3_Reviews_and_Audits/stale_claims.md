## Reusable wording blocks (referenced as [R-xxx] in the tables)

**[R-RAIN]** Rainfall file j is the rain that falls during the 30 minutes [1800 j, 1800 (j+1)) s of the simulation, and the values are mm/h intensities (the domain-mean depth gain over an interval is 0.494-0.502 x the file value; correlation 1.00 at lag 0 in all four regions; `completion/a_rain_lag.py`). The original loader (the "hybrid" input) gave each input frame at time t the file floor(t/1800), the interval that contains t, so the last observed frame carried up to 30 minutes of rain that had not yet fallen. Depth inputs contain no future information; rainfall inputs did. "Causal" inputs use only completed intervals (file floor(t/1800) - 1); "forced" inputs add the rain of the five forecast intervals (a rainfall-forced surrogate, the analogue of the paper's FNO+).

**[R-BASE]** A per-pixel linear model that sees only the depth history scores skill +0.15 (480 m) to +0.35 (30 m) against persistence, more than the original v1 CNN-LSTM (+0.05 to +0.23); a per-pixel MLP with no convolution and no recurrence scores about +0.84 on the never-seen UK and Mozambique, and a 4,101-parameter local CNN about +0.80 to +0.84. Beating persistence is therefore a low bar. (The v1 versus linear comparison uses different window sets and folds until batch C, v1 under the same folds, has run: [NUMBER PENDING].)

**[R-MECH]** In v1, test-time removal of rainfall took skill to about zero and scrambling or freezing the depth history changed it by less than 0.01. These are properties of v1, not of the flood. v1 could not use the depth history (each frame is squeezed through a 128-number dense layer and depth is divided by 5 m), and its last-frame rain channel partly held rain that had not yet fallen. In the newer models freezing or scrambling the depth history destroys the skill, while removing rainfall or terrain barely matters (local CNN on Mozambique: 0.80 to 0.78 without rain). Whether convolution or recurrence adds anything beyond a per-pixel model is [PENDING: kernel ablation K, clstm versus local].

**[R-PAPER]** The paper's metric named "RMSE" is a relative (normalised) error, not metres. Its task is whole-domain: 19 steps at 300 s (95 minutes) from the true depth at t = 1 of each 20-frame sample (FNO+ also receives the terrain and the rainfall series). Under the paper's own protocol and metrics plain persistence (copy the t = 1 frame) scores NSE 0.9995 / CSI(1 mm) 0.983 on Mozambique (whole event) against FNO+ 0.955 / 0.934, and on the UK at 60 m NSE 0.9993 / CSI 0.980 against FNO+ 0.9989 / 0.860. The paper reports no persistence baseline. Our patch model is not directly comparable to the paper's tables; persistence is.

**[R-GEO]** The world files describe the 30 m base grid. For the 60 m and 480 m grids the recovered scale and crop offset are applied on top. The result was checked against OpenStreetMap for Mozambique (480 m), Pakistan (480 m) and the UK (60 m): rivers, lakes, coast and river meanders match at kilometre to tens-of-metres precision (`completion/results/georef_*.png`). Australia at 60 m and the UK at 30 m were not separately overlaid; Australia at 30 m was checked earlier against satellite imagery. (The check script applies the offset; the notebook's export cell still handles Australia 30 m only.)

**[R-TAG]** Prefix for every result from the original model: "v1 (original CNN-LSTM, hybrid rain input)". Suffix for anything about severity, rules or Grad-CAM: "v1 joint model; re-evaluation under the final protocol [NUMBER PENDING]".
